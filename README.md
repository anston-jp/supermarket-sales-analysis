# Supermarket Sales Performance & Operational Analytics

## 🌐 Overview
This project delivers a comprehensive exploratory data analysis of supermarket branch performance to support strategic retail decision-making. Using historical transaction data spanning multiple branches, this analysis evaluates revenue distribution, product line profitability, customer satisfaction ratings, and preferred payment channels. The final deliverable features a fully interactive Excel dashboard equipped with dynamic pivot tables and KPIs to track business health at a glance.

## 📂 Dataset
* **Source:** Historical supermarket sales dataset containing branch transactions.
* **Volume:** 1,000 rows of transactional records across 16 descriptive features.
* **Key Attributes:** Invoice ID, Branch, City, Customer type, Gender, Product line, Unit price, Quantity, Tax, Total sales, Date, Time, Payment method, Cost of goods sold (COGS), Gross margin percentage, Gross income, and Customer Rating.

* **Data Visualization:** Excel Dashboards, Slicers, Bar Charts, and KPI summary cards.

## 🎯 Business Objectives
The analysis was structured around core operational questions to guide retail management:
1. **Revenue Performance:** Which city branch generated the highest total revenue?
2. **Product Dominance:** Which product lines drove the highest sales across different locations and contributed the most gross income?
3. **Customer Sentiment:** What are the average customer satisfaction ratings across various product categories?
4. **Transaction Habits:** What are the most frequently used customer payment methods?
5. **Sales Trends:** How do sales trend over the recorded operational period?

---

## 📊 Key Findings
* **Branch Revenue Leadership:** **Naypyitaw** emerged as the top-performing branch, generating **$110,568.71** in total revenue, closely followed by Yangon ($106,200.37) and Mandalay ($106,197.67).
* **Product Line Profitability:** **Food and Beverages** generated the highest overall gross income (**$2,673.56**), followed closely by Sports and Travel ($2,624.90). 
* **City-Specific Best Sellers:** 
  * *Mandalay:* Sports and travel ($19,988.20)
  * *Naypyitaw:* Food and beverages ($23,766.86)
  * *Yangon:* Home and lifestyle ($22,417.20)
* **Customer Ratings:** Product satisfaction ratings remained consistent across all categories, with **Food and Beverages** securing the highest average rating at **7.11 / 10**, and Home and Lifestyle recording the lowest at **6.84 / 10**.
* **Payment Methods:** **E-wallets** are narrowly the most preferred payment option (345 transactions), closely tracked by Cash (344 transactions) and Credit Cards (311 transactions), indicating a balanced digital and physical payment ecosystem.

---

## 📈 Executive Summary
The supermarket chain demonstrates stable, balanced revenue distribution across its three operating cities (Mandalay, Naypyitaw, and Yangon), totaling over **$322,966 in gross sales** with an aggregate gross income of **$15,379.37**. While regional revenues are neck-and-neck, product category performance reveals distinct customer preference variations by location. Profitability is heavily anchored in consumable and lifestyle goods, while customer payment preferences show near-equal adoption of modern digital e-wallets and traditional cash methods.

---

## 💡 Recommendations
* **Targeted Inventory Allocation:** Stock up on *Food and Beverages* heavily in the Naypyitaw branch and *Sports & Travel* inventory in Mandalay to match localized peak demand.
* **Promote Digital Payment Adoption:** Given that E-wallets lead transaction volume slightly, introduce loyalty cashback incentives for digital wallet checkouts to reduce cash-handling overhead.
* **Quality Assurance in Home & Lifestyle:** Investigate the factors behind the lower customer ratings (6.84) in the Home & Lifestyle category to improve customer satisfaction and repeat purchases.

---

## ⚠️ Limitations
* **Time Horizon:** The dataset covers a limited timeframe, preventing multi-year longitudinal trend analysis or seasonal YoY comparisons.
* **External Factors:** Macroeconomic indicators such as local competitor pricing, regional marketing campaigns, and holiday impacts were not captured in the transactional logs.
